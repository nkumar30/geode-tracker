# USA Buzzer Practice — Nov 24

Here are **25 pyramidal tossup questions** focused on United States geography, written in the standard national-level academic competition format (progressing from challenging early clues to mid-level context, followed by giveaways).

---

### Question 1
**Category: Counties**  
This jurisdiction contains the Gila Bend Mountains and the McDowell Sonoran Preserve, and its southern portion includes the municipal boundary of Gila Bend. Spanning 9,224 square miles—larger than Connecticut, Delaware, and Rhode Island combined—it borders six other counties, including La Paz, Yuma, Yavapai, and Pinal. It represents the second-largest single voting jurisdiction in the United States by population, trailing only Los Angeles County, and encompasses the Salt River Valley. For 10 points, name this most populous county in Arizona, with its seat at Phoenix.  
**ANSWER:** **Maricopa** County

---

### Question 2
**Category: Rivers**  
The headwaters of this river issue from Jackson Lake in Grand Teton National Park before cutting through the Palisades Reservoir and incising a massive quaternary basalt canyon across the Magic Valley. Shoshone Falls, often termed the "Niagara of the West," drops this river by 212 feet near Twin Falls. Further downstream, it forms the deepest river gorge in North America along Hells Canyon on the Oregon border before joining the Columbia River at the Tri-Cities. For 10 points, name this primary river of southern Idaho.  
**ANSWER:** **Snake** River

---

### Question 3
**Category: Landforms & Physiographic Regions**  
Bounded on the north by the Canadian River valley and on the east by the Caprock Escarpment, this physiographic section of the Great Plains represents one of the largest mesas in North America. Francisco Vázquez de Coronado crossed this region in 1541, noting that travelers required stakes driven into the soil to mark their paths across its almost featureless shortgrass terrain. Underlain by the southern reach of the Ogallala Aquifer, its major urban centers include Lubbock and Amarillo. For 10 points, identify this semi-arid plateau spanning eastern New Mexico and northwestern Texas, whose Spanish name translates to "Staked Plains."  
**ANSWER:** **Llano Estacado** (prompt on *Staked Plains*)

---

### Question 4
**Category: Lakes**  
This lake is separated from Whitefish Bay by Point Iroquois, and its Keweenaw Current drives cold, nutrient-poor waters clockwise around a central peninsula rich in native copper deposits. Isle Royale sits within its northwestern waters near the international boundary, and the St. Marys River drains its waters through the Soo Locks into Lake Huron. Holding more water than all the other Great Lakes combined, it represents the largest freshwater lake by surface area in the world. For 10 points, name this deepest and northernmost Great Lake.  
**ANSWER:** Lake **Superior**

---

### Question 5
**Category: Forests & Public Lands**  
This temperate rainforest is home to Admiralty Island—known locally as *Kootznoowoo*, or "Fortress of the Bears"—and the Alexander Archipelago. Covering roughly 16.7 million acres, it encompasses the Mendenhall Glacier and is managed primarily out of regional headquarters in Ketchikan and Juneau. It is the single largest administrative national forest in the United States, spanning most of the Alaska Panhandle. For 10 points, name this massive Southeast Alaska national forest.  
**ANSWER:** **Tongass** National Forest

---

### Question 6
**Category: Cities**  
Located at the mouth of the St. Louis River, this city was laid out along a massive freshwater tombolo known as Minnesota Point. Situated at the westernmost terminus of the Saint Lawrence Seaway, it became a major transshipment hub for taconite pellets extracted from the nearby Vermilion and Mesabi iron ranges. Connected across the harbor to Superior, Wisconsin, by the Blatnik Bridge and Richard I. Bong Memorial Bridge, it is home to an iconic Aerial Lift Bridge. For 10 points, name this major port city in northeastern Minnesota on the western tip of Lake Superior.  
**ANSWER:** **Duluth**

---

### Question 7
**Category: Landforms & Physiography**  
This physical feature was formed during the late Cretaceous and Tertiary periods as an antecedent stream maintained its course by incising downward through the quartzites of Kittatinny Mountain faster than the surrounding ridge uplifted. Located along the border between Warren and Sussex counties in New Jersey and Monroe and Northampton counties in Pennsylvania, it separates Mount Tammany from Mount Minsi. For 10 points, name this prominent water gap carved through the Appalachian Valley and Ridge province by the Delaware River.  
**ANSWER:** **Delaware Water Gap**

---

### Question 8
**Category: Counties**  
This county contains the Kelso Dunes, the Bristol Dry Lake bed, and the Bullion Mountains inside the Marine Corps Air Ground Combat Center at Twentynine Palms. Spanning over 20,105 square miles, it encompasses major portions of the Mojave National Preserve and Joshua Tree National Park and extends eastward to the Colorado River at Needles. Covering more area than each of the nine smallest U.S. states, for 10 points, name this geographically largest county in the contiguous United States, located in southern California.  
**ANSWER:** **San Bernardino** County

---

### Question 9
**Category: Rivers**  
This river rises near Blue Ridge Lake and flows through the Chattahoochee-Oconee National Forests before entering Lake Lanier behind Buford Dam. Forming the southern half of the border between Georgia and Alabama, it cuts through the fall line at Columbus before joining the Flint River at Lake Seminole to form the Apalachicola River. Subject to decades of interstate water-allocation litigation between Georgia, Alabama, and Florida, for 10 points, name this river that serves as the primary municipal water source for metropolitan Atlanta.  
**ANSWER:** **Chattahoochee** River

---

### Question 10
**Category: Physiographic Regions**  
This distinct area occupies parts of four states and is characterized by steep, dissected coulees and deeply carved dendritic valleys that lack glacial drift, kettle lakes, or terminal moraines. Because it was bypassed by successive lobes of the Laurentide Ice Sheet—specifically the Green Bay and Des Moines lobes—its karst landscape preserves pre-Pleistocene topography along the Upper Mississippi River. Centered around towns like La Crosse and Dubuque, for 10 points, identify this unglaciated region occupying southwestern Wisconsin, southeastern Minnesota, northeastern Iowa, and northwestern Illinois.  
**ANSWER:** **Driftless Area** (or **Paleozoic Plateau**)

---

### Question 11
**Category: Lakes**  
Fed primarily by the Kissimmee River, this shallow water body rests in a limestone depression that historically discharged into the Everglades through a slow-moving sheet of water known as the "River of Grass." Catastrophic hurricane storm surges in 1926 and 1928 led the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers to encircle it with the Herbert Hoover Dike. Spanning 730 square miles with an average depth of under 10 feet, for 10 points, name this largest freshwater lake in Florida.  
**ANSWER:** Lake **Okeechobee**

---

### Question 12
**Category: Mountain Ranges & Provinces**  
Unlike the north-south alignment typical of the adjacent Appalachian ranges, this physiographic province features an unusual east-west structural trend resulting from late Paleozoic compression during the Ouachita orogeny. Home to Mount Magazine—the highest point in its home state—and extensive deposits of novaculite, it is separated from the Ozark Plateaus to the north by the Arkansas River Valley. For 10 points, name this mountain range spanning western Arkansas and southeastern Oklahoma, famous for the thermal springs at Hot Springs National Park.  
**ANSWER:** **Ouachita** Mountains [WASH-i-tah]

---

### Question 13
**Category: Counties**  
Containing the tribal lands of the Havasupai and the communities of Fredonia and Page, this county is bounded on the north by the Utah state line along the 37th parallel. It encompasses the volcanic San Francisco Peaks—including Humphreys Peak, the highest elevation in Arizona—and the Marble Canyon section of the Colorado River. Spanning 18,661 square miles, for 10 points, name this second-largest county in the contiguous United States, with its seat at Flagstaff and encompassing the majority of Grand Canyon National Park.  
**ANSWER:** **Coconino** County

---

### Question 14
**Category: Rivers**  
This river rises on the western slopes of the Continental Divide in the Black Range of Sierra County, New Mexico, before flowing across the Gila Cliff Dwellings National Monument and cutting through Safford Valley. Dammed at Coolidge Dam to form the San Carlos Reservoir, it historically supported the agricultural civilization of the Hohokam people before modern irrigation diverts virtually all its lower discharge upstream of Yuma. For 10 points, name this 649-mile-long tributary of the Colorado River that forms much of the historic southern boundary established by the 1853 Gadsden Purchase.  
**ANSWER:** **Gila** River [HEE-luh]

---

### Question 15
**Category: Bays & Estuaries**  
Formed during the Holocene epoch as rising sea levels drowned the lower valleys of the Susquehanna and James rivers, this body of water is divided between an Upper and Lower Eastern Shore. Protected by Cape Charles and Cape Henry at its southern Virginia capes, it suffers from recurring seasonal anoxia caused by nutrient runoff across a 64,000-square-mile watershed. For 10 points, name this largest estuary in the United States, famous for blue crabs and spanned by a landmark bridge-tunnel connecting Norfolk to the Delmarva Peninsula.  
**ANSWER:** **Chesapeake** Bay

---

### Question 16
**Category: Landforms & Uplifts**  
This elliptical geologic dome was produced during the Laramide Orogeny when an uplift of Precambrian granites and schists punched through overlying Paleozoic and Mesozoic sedimentary beds, leaving a surrounding ring of hogbacks known as the Red Valley or "Race Track." Flanked by the Belle Fourche and Cheyenne rivers, it contains Harney Peak (Black Elk Peak) and the world's longest cave systems at Jewel Cave and Wind Cave. For 10 points, name this isolated mountain uplift in western South Dakota and northeastern Wyoming that features Mount Rushmore.  
**ANSWER:** **Black Hills** (or *Pahá Sápa*)

---

### Question 17
**Category: Forests & Public Lands**  
This public reserve encloses three distinct rain-forest valleys—the Hoh, Queets, and Quinault—which receive over 140 inches of annual precipitation from Pacific maritime fronts. Surrounding an eponymous national park containing Mount Olympus, it is situated south of the Strait of Juan de Fuca and east of the Pacific Ocean. For 10 points, name this national forest situated on the Olympic Peninsula of northwestern Washington.  
**ANSWER:** **Olympic** National Forest

---

### Question 18
**Category: Counties**  
Bordering the Canadian provinces of Quebec to the west and north and New Brunswick to the east, this jurisdiction encompasses the drainage basin of the Fish River and the northern reaches of the Allagash Wilderness Waterway. Covering 6,828 square miles, it is colloquially known simply as "The County" and is celebrated as the premier agricultural center of northern New England, leading its state in potato cultivation. For 10 points, name this geographically largest county in Maine, with its administrative seat at Houlton.  
**ANSWER:** **Aroostook** County

---

### Question 19
**Category: Lakes**  
Connected to the Hudson River by a historic 60-mile barge canal via Whitehall, this body of water contains Grand Isle and Isle La Motte, which preserve the Ordovician Chazy fossil reef. During the War of 1812, American naval forces under Thomas Macdonough defeated a British squadron in its Plattsburgh Bay. Serving as a partial boundary between New York and Vermont, for 10 points, name this 120-mile-long lake nestled between the Adirondack and Green Mountains.  
**ANSWER:** Lake **Champlain**

---

### Question 20
**Category: Cities**  
Located at the confluence of the Willamette and Columbia rivers, this city was platted on a site originally known as "The Clearing." It developed rapidly as an export terminal for wheat grown in the Palouse and lumber from the Cascade Range. Dominated visually by Mount Hood, it is bisected into east and west sections by the Willamette River and is noted for its early implementation of an urban growth boundary to prevent suburban sprawl. For 10 points, name this most populous city in Oregon, nicknamed the "City of Roses."  
**ANSWER:** **Portland**

---

### Question 21
**Category: Waterways & Swamps**  
This wetland is centered on Lake Drummond, a naturally circular freshwater lake situated on the Suffolk Scarp. Surveyed by George Washington in 1763, who formed an eponymous land company to drain its bald cypress and Atlantic white cedar stands, it long served as a refuge for maroon communities of self-emancipated formerly enslaved people. Spanning the coastal plain border between southeastern Virginia and northeastern North Carolina, for 10 points, identify this vast protected wetland.  
**ANSWER:** **Great Dismal Swamp**

---

### Question 22
**Category: Rivers**  
This river is formed at Fairmont, West Virginia, by the confluence of the West Fork and Tygart rivers, and it flows northward across the unglaciated Allegheny Plateau. Historically flanked by the heavy coke works and blast furnaces of towns like McKeesport, Charleroi, and Homestead, it joins the Allegheny River at Point State Park. For 10 points, name this northward-flowing river that unites with the Allegheny at Pittsburgh to form the Ohio River.  
**ANSWER:** **Monongahela** River

---

### Question 23
**Category: Plateaus & Physiography**  
This deeply dissected plateau is bounded on the east by an abrupt 1,000-foot escarpment known as Walden Ridge and the Cumberland Escarpment, and on the west by the Highland Rim of the Interior Low Plateaus. Rich in bituminous coal seams and karst sinkholes, it contains the Big South Fork National River and Fall Creek Falls. For 10 points, identify this southern division of the Appalachian Plateaus province that extends southward across eastern Kentucky, Tennessee, and northern Alabama.  
**ANSWER:** **Cumberland Plateau**

---

### Question 24
**Category: Counties**  
This county contains the Skokie Lagoons, the Des Plaines River Trail, and the Calumet Sag Channel, an engineering canal that reversed the flow of the Calumet River into the Illinois Waterway. Established in 1831 and named for early statesman Daniel Pope Cook, it borders Lake Michigan on the east and DuPage and Lake counties on the west and north. With over five million residents, it ranks as the second-most populous county in the entire nation. For 10 points, name this county in northeastern Illinois whose seat is Chicago.  
**ANSWER:** **Cook** County

---

### Question 25
**Category: Lakes**  
This terminal endorheic lake occupies an active graben within the Salton Trough, which sits over 220 feet below sea level. It was accidentally formed in 1905 when Colorado River floodwaters breached wooden irrigation headgates of the California Development Company and flowed uncontrolled into the Alamo and New River channels for nearly two years. Formerly a thriving mid-century resort destination in the Imperial Valley, for 10 points, name this hypersaline lake in southern California.  
**ANSWER:** **Salton Sea**
